# Copy decisions

## Hero headline

**Chosen:** "Home-cooked Habesha food, made by *your neighbors*."

Leads with authenticity ("home-cooked") and closes on the community/economic-
empowerment story ("your neighbors") in one line — the two pillars of the
brief. Matches the approved mockup exactly, which was itself the design
reference this build was told to follow.

**Alternates considered:**

1. "Real Habesha cooking, from a kitchen near you."
   Leans harder on proximity/discovery, but "near you" reads close to the
   generic food-delivery-app framing the brief explicitly wants to avoid.

2. "Your neighbor's kitchen. Doro wat included."
   Punchier and more specific — naming a dish sells appetite immediately —
   but assumes the reader already knows what Doro Wat is, which is a bad
   bet for a headline whose job is to work in the first five seconds for
   *anyone* who lands on the page, not just people already fluent in the
   cuisine.

## Waitlist success copy

- Client, first-time: "You're on the list. We haven't forgotten you — we're
  coming." (celebratory, matches the mockup's tone)
- Client/cook, duplicate email: "You're already on the list — we haven't
  forgotten you." (per brief — graceful, not an error)
- Cook, first-time: "You're on the list. We'll reach out as we open your
  city." (distinct from the client message since cook onboarding is a
  higher-touch process, not just a notify-me signal)

## Amharic (Fidel) accents

Kept intentionally minimal: the single word "እንኳን ደህና መጡ" (welcome, in the
hero) and dish names in the dishes grid — both taken directly from the
brief's own examples. An early draft of the waitlist section included an
invented full Amharic sentence translating "we haven't forgotten you — we're
coming to your city"; it was cut because I'm not confident enough in that
grammar to publish it on a site targeting Habesha readers. If you have a
correct phrase you'd like used there, drop it in and I'll wire it in as a
`font-ethiopic` accent the same way the others are done.
